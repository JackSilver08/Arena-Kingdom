# Arena Kingdom Map V2

## Final battlefield concept

The match is played on one large island surrounded by the ocean.

- **Blue Kingdom:** left half of the island
- **Red Kingdom:** right half of the island
- **Symmetry:** Red coordinates are an exact horizontal mirror of Blue coordinates
- **Neutral strip:** a band of open grass between the two kingdoms where nobody can build
- **No bridges:** troops can cross anywhere along the front line
- **Terrain:** open ground with woods and low hills that are purely decorative
- **No obstacles:** no roads, trees, rocks, or decorative blockers

## Gameplay geometry

The coastline in `shared/src/island.ts` is the single source of truth: troops walk anywhere on the island, and the drawn shore is exactly where they stop. It is a rounded rectangle (X 160–1760, Y 148–932) pushed 12–82 px outwards by a wave of cosines, which makes it an exact mirror image across X = 960.

| Area | X | Y |
|---|---:|---:|
| Blue territory (buildable) | shore – 830 | shore to shore |
| Neutral strip | 830–1090 | shore to shore |
| Red territory (buildable) | 1090 – shore | shore to shore |

- Buildings must stand wholly on land on their own side of the front line.
- A fence only needs 8 px of its length on land, so it may run out over the beach. A chain of snapped fences started anywhere along a column can always be continued until it seals the far shore, so a player can wall off the entire front line with no gap to slip around.

## Starting structures

Each side starts with:

- 1 Castle
- 3 Villages
- 1 Barracks
- 3 Troops

The Red layout is generated by mirroring the Blue layout across X = 960. This guarantees competitive symmetry.

## Visual philosophy

The battlefield is drawn as an **old military campaign map**, with the kingdoms' pieces standing on it like a war table:

- aged paper with stains, grain, fold creases and a darkened vignette
- a hand-inked coastline with engraved water lines around the island
- woods marked with tree symbols, hills with contour rings and spot heights; both are mirrored so neither kingdom gets different-looking terrain
- a lettered and numbered grid (A–P, 1–9), a graduated neatline, compass rose, scale bar and title in the margin
- dashed front lines in blue and red ink mark where each kingdom may build, with no man's land between them
- the drawn coastline always lies outside the walkable rectangle, so every reachable point looks like land
- all gameplay logic remains independent from the renderer
